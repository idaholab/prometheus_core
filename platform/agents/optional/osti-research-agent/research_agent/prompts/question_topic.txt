You name what a paper's title or abstract must mention to be worth reading
for a research question.

QUESTION:
{question}

Reply with the JSON object alone, starting with "{{", even when a name in
the question looks unknown or invented:
{{"subject": ["<name>", ...], "quantity": ["<name>", ...]}}

"subject": the specific thing the question is about: a material or alloy, a
fuel form, a code or model, a benchmark or project, or a named method. A
paper whose title and abstract name none of these is not read, so list it
only when the question names one such thing. Leave it empty when the
question asks about a class or a field instead, such as "nickel
superalloys", "ferritic steels", "ceramics", "fast reactor fuels",
"neutronics codes" or "deep learning". A test, measurement or analysis
method asked about (tensile testing, sensitivity analysis) is the quantity,
not the subject. When the subject is one thing, list every name a paper
about it would use:
- the name as asked, its abbreviations and designations, and other grades
  of the same alloy: for 347 stainless steel also 347H, SS347, AISI 347,
  UNS S34700; for Hastelloy N also Alloy N, INOR-8; for ThO2 also thorium
  dioxide, thoria;
- for a benchmark, project, code or reactor design, the short names papers
  use for it as well as the full one: for the OECD/NEA Kalinin-3 coupled
  benchmark also "Kalinin-3" and "Kalinin"; for the MSRE also "Molten Salt
  Reactor Experiment". A long official name is rarely written out word for
  word, so never list only long names;
- but never a wider class that would let in papers about other things: not
  "stainless steel" for 347, not "nickel alloy" for Hastelloy N, not
  "oxide fuel" for ThO2.
A question that names two such things (U-Zr fuel in the MARMOT code) lists
names for both: a paper naming either is read. At most 10 names.

"quantity": the property, phenomenon or result asked about, with its common
variants: yield strength, 0.2% proof stress; void swelling, swelling; decay
heat uncertainty, decay heat. At most 8 names.
- Not the conditions it is asked under or as a function of: for "yield
  strength of ThO2 versus temperature", temperature is a condition; for
  "swelling after neutron irradiation", irradiation is.
- Not words that fit almost any paper: review, experiments, conclusion,
  analysis, results.

Leave a list empty when the question does not name one, for example a
question about a broad field or one that never says what "it" is. Use only
names of what the question asks about and their standard equivalents, with
no numbers of conditions or units.
